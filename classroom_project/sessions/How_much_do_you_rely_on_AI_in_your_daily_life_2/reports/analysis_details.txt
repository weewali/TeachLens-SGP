6. Limitations
- Behaviour labels come from rule-based heuristics on pose and COCO object cues, not from trained behaviour models, and they have not been checked against human labels.
- The two cameras were lined up by the recording-start time in each video's metadata, about +/-1 s (the student camera started 8.0 s before the instructor camera). A moment can be off by about one sample (2 s).
- The instructor is the most confident person inside the instructor camera's teacher zone. If the instructor walks into the student camera's view, they are counted as a student there.
- Facing the class means the instructor's face was visible to the instructor camera, which assumes that camera sits on the students' side of the room.
- No board region was calibrated, so time at the board was not measured.
- Seats are the places where a student sat in at least 10% of the samples, so a student who sat somewhere only briefly is left out, one who changes seats counts as two seats, and nobody is identified.
- Writing cannot be told apart from looking down, so note-taking may count as off-task.
- 'Head down and still' (head down or face hidden, almost no movement, 30 s or more) often means resting or sleeping, but quiet reading or writing can look the same.
- Phones are small objects that are easily missed or confused with other objects, and a phone may be used for course work.
- Frames are sampled every 2 s, so short actions can be missed.
